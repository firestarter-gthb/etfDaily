# The Annual Cycle: 24 OpEx Periods & Seasonality Pulse

*An empirical analysis of the S&P 500 (SPY 1993 – 2026) across 12 monthly options expirations and 24 half-cycles*

---

## 1. Introduction: Why 24 Expiration Periods?

Traditional market seasonality almost exclusively analyzes the **calendar month**: 1st to 30th/31st. While this feels intuitive, it fails to match actual institutional money flows on Wall Street.

Major institutional rebalancing, derivatives positioning, options rollovers, and dealer gamma dynamics are anchored around **monthly options expiration (OpEx)**: the **3rd Friday of every month**.

In this empirical study, we segment the trading year into **12 contiguous OpEx cycles** (3rd Friday to 3rd Friday), and split each cycle into two halves of roughly 10 trading days:
1. **1st Half (1H: Post-OpEx & Month-Start)**: From the prior month's 3rd Friday to cycle midpoint (days 1 to ~10).
2. **2nd Half (2H: Pre-OpEx & Expiration Run)**: From cycle midpoint to the new 3rd Friday (days ~11 to ~20).

Together, this produces **24 sequential periods**, uncovering the granular seasonal pulse of the U.S. stock market over 33 years of data (SPY 1993–2026).
